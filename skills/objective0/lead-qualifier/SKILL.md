# Lead qualifier

Turn a company name, or a list of them, into a decision: is this a customer worth your time now, a good fit for later, or not a fit? It protects the most expensive thing a startup has, which is the founders' time. A company that looks interesting but has no money to spend is not qualified, and the verdict says so plainly.

## First: your ideal customer

Before any research, get the person's bar, one question at a time. Skip anything they've already said.

1. **Who do you sell to?** Industry, kind of business (B2B or B2C), size or stage, location.
2. **Who has to be in the conversation?** The role that buys (a founder, a head of sales, a CFO).
3. **How do you know they can pay?** For example: raised at least a set amount in the last 6 months, or revenue above a set level, or a budget line for this. Pick one money test.
4. **What signals help?** A recent raise, hiring for a certain role, a competitor's customer, a pilot already running.
5. **What rules a company out?** Too small, wrong market, a competitor, in trouble.

Write the bar back as a short list and confirm it. Use the same bar for every company in the run.

## Two modes

- **One company (default).** Full research, a research doc, a one-page briefing.
- **A list.** A lighter pass on each: what they do, the money test, traction, a distress check and a one-line reason. One ranked table, best first. Offer the full version for the top few.

## Step 1: distress, then the money test

**Distress, always first.** Search the last 18 months for insolvency, administration, winding-up petitions, layoffs, a founder leaving, a down round or litigation. For UK companies check recent Companies House filings. Distress overrides everything else and leads the verdict.

**The money test**, answered before anything else and put first in every output: does the company pass the person's money test? Yes, no or unverified, with the evidence and a link.

- Only a closed, announced round counts as a raise. Not a raise they're seeking, total raised over the company's life, a grant or an accelerator place.
- "Public" means a dated source you can link: a press release, a news article, a filing, the investor's announcement. A database headline with no date is not enough.
- **Raised longer ago than the test allows:** estimate whether the money is still there (the method is in [references/scoring.md](references/scoring.md)), and show the working.
- **Can't verify:** write "unverified", treat the test as not passed, and give the exact question to ask them.
- Revenue can stand in for a raise only when it's clearly enough to pay. Show the numbers.

A clear need never makes up for a failed money test.

## Step 2: the rest of the research

Use web search, the company site, company registries, funding databases, LinkedIn, and anything the person already has. Link every source. Mark anything you couldn't check as unverified.

- **What they do**, in plain words: what they sell, to whom, and how they make money.
- **Legal entity:** registered name, company number, date and country.
- **Funding in full:** every round with type, amount, date and investors, and whether they're raising now.
- **Who you're talking to:** name, role, background, and whether they're the buyer.
- **Traction:** paying customers, live pilots, pipeline. Be specific.
- **Hiring:** open roles, and what they suggest about what the company needs now.

## Step 3: the verdict

Read [references/scoring.md](references/scoring.md) before rating.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Qualified | Passes the money test, every rule on the bar is true, nothing rules it out |
| 🟡 Good fit, not ready | The right kind of company with a likely need, but the money test is failed or unverified |
| 🔴 Not a fit | Outside the bar, no need you can help with, or in distress |

State the verdict, the one-line reason, what's unverified (with the exact question that would settle it) and the next step.

## Step 4: write it up

For one company, two documents from the templates in `references/`: the full workup ([research-doc-template.md](references/research-doc-template.md)) and the one-page briefing for whoever takes the call ([one-pager-template.md](references/one-pager-template.md)). Both start with the verdict and the money test line. For a list, one ranked table: company, what they do, money test, traction, verdict, reason.

Never add a company to a CRM, send anything or contact anyone unless the person asks, and show exactly what you'd write first.

## What good looks like

A good one-pager starts "🟡 Good fit, not ready. Money test: no. Raised a £1.2M seed in March 2025 (link), about 14 months ago, with a team of 18: at a rough £8k a month per person, that money ran out around now. Ask: 'Have you raised since, or are you profitable?'" Every claim has a link, the unverified ones are marked, and the verdict follows the bar the person set.

A weak one calls a company qualified because it "looks exciting", counts a raise it's still seeking, treats an undated database figure as fact, or skips the distress check.
