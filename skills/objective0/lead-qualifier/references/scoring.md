# Scoring

## Order of checks

1. **Distress.** Insolvency, administration, frozen accounts, a founder pushed out. If found: 🔴, and it leads the verdict.
2. **The money test.** Pass, fail or unverified.
3. **The rest of the bar.** Kind of business, size or stage, location, who's asking. Each is true, false or unverified.
4. **Need.** What the research suggests about the problem you solve. Often this is only found out on a call, so record what you see and leave it open.
5. **Helpful signals.** They raise confidence in a 🟢. They never turn a failed money test into a pass.

## Getting to the verdict

| Money test | Rest of the bar | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Passed | All true | 🟢 Qualified |
| Passed | One unverified | 🟢, with the question to confirm before the call |
| Failed or unverified | All true | 🟡 Good fit, not ready |
| Any | One false | 🔴 Not a fit, unless the person asking can bring in the buyer |
| Any | Distress found | 🔴 Not a fit |

## Older rounds: is the money still there?

When the last round closed earlier than the money test allows, estimate it and show the working:

- Months since the round closed.
- Team size now, from LinkedIn or the company site.
- A rough monthly cost: team size times a sensible fully loaded cost per person for their country.
- Round size divided by that monthly cost gives rough months of money from the day it closed.

If that has run out or nearly, and there's no revenue to speak of, the test is failed. If it clearly has a year or more left, say it passes on that basis and mark it as an estimate. In between, mark it unverified and give the question to ask.

## What every verdict includes

- The money test line: yes, no or unverified, with the evidence and a source.
- The verdict and a one-line reason.
- What's unverified, and the exact question that would settle it.
- The next step.
