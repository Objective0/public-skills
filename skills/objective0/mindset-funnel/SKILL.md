# The Mindset Funnel

A way to diagnose user activation, based on the Mindset Funnel by **Diego de Jódar** (*Please Don't Push*). The idea is his; this skill applies it to your product with you. Read his work for the full thinking (sources at the end).

## The idea

A classic funnel treats activation as one event: a signup, or a first action. Diego's point is that activation is really weeks of using the product and running out of blockers along the way. So the stages are defined by what the user is trying to figure out, and what's stopping them, not by what the company wants them to do.

"Being activated" (early and fragile) is different from "hard activated" (every blocker cleared, so they stay). Teams often mistake the first for the second.

| Stage | What the user is asking | Typical blockers | What to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Exploring** (before signup) | "Does this solve my problem?" | Unclear message, weak positioning, can't see themselves in it | Visits |
| **2. Trying** (signup to first real use) | "Does it actually work?" | Confusing start, no obvious first step, doesn't deliver what was promised | First meaningful action |
| **3. Integrating** (early repeat use) | "Can I fit this into how I work?" | Clashes with habits or tools, rare use case, gets complicated with more use | Several core actions (often 5 to 10) |
| **4. Using** (habit forming) | "Does it work every time?" | Edge cases, formats that don't fit, integrations that break or get forgotten | Steady sessions over time |
| **5. Hard activation** | It's automatic and essential | None left | Regular use, indefinitely |

**Pull, don't push.** You can push users down the funnel with tactics (nag emails, gamification, incentives) while the blockers stay, and the numbers look good until acquisition slows. Or you can pull: find and remove the real blocker at each stage, so users want to move on. Stop asking "how do we raise conversion?" and ask "why are users stuck, and what's blocking them?"

## How to run it

One question at a time. Ask for anything that would help: funnel numbers, cohort data, onboarding screens, support tickets, interview notes, churn reasons.

1. **The product and the user.** What it does, who uses it, and what "using it properly" looks like. Is value from one use or from repeated use?
2. **Pick the measure for each stage.** For a single-use tool, specific core actions; for an app people come back to, sessions. Agree a threshold for "integrating" (how many core actions).
3. **Map where users are.** For each recent cohort, roughly how many reach each stage. Where's the biggest drop?
4. **Find the blocker at that stage.** The specific reason people don't move on. Push past the first answer: "how do you know?" Look for evidence from users who stayed and users who left: the contrast shows blockers the numbers hide. If there's no evidence, the first step is to talk to five of each.
5. **Decide the fix, as a pull.** Remove the blocker rather than push past it. One stage, one blocker, one fix at a time. Check it doesn't break something elsewhere: product, marketing and sales all touch activation.
6. **Look at friction and incentives.** Some early friction filters for real intent (a longer signup form can mean better users); don't remove it by reflex. Do the team's incentives reward activation, or only the signup or the closed deal?

## What you get

- The five stages for this product, with the measure and threshold for each.
- Where users are stuck now, with the evidence.
- The blocker at the worst stage, how sure we are, and what would make us surer.
- One fix to try first, written as a test: what we change, what we expect to see, and by when.
- Any "push" tactics in use that are hiding a blocker.

## Where it fits best

Startups before Series B with a subscription or freemium product, B2B or B2C, where repeated use drives the value and the team can still change course.

## What good looks like

"Most users stall at Trying: 62% sign up and never create a second project. In the eight churn interviews, six said they couldn't see where to start. Fix to test first: open new accounts on a filled-in example project. We expect over 40% to reach a second project within a week, read after two weeks." The stage is named, the evidence is real, and the fix removes a blocker.

A weak run says "improve onboarding" or "send more emails", has no evidence for the blocker, or treats a signup as activation.
