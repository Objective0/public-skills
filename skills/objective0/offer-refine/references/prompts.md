# The four steps

Run these as steps in the conversation, not as a script to paste. They come from a popular Hormozi prompt set, tidied up.

## The variables

Fill these first, from what the person gives you. Never write them back into the output as `{NAME}` and so on.

- **NAME:** the offer's name.
- **BUSINESS:** what the seller does and who they are, in a few lines.
- **PRODUCT:** everything in the offer, as a list.
- **TARGET_MARKET:** the person they want to reach.
- **The equation:** Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice).

## Prompt 1: be the buyer

> Your first goal is to pretend to be my TARGET_MARKET. Describe your goals and dream outcomes, how your market is being disrupted, and the challenges with competition. Then describe the obstacles in the way of your dream outcomes, and the pain points. Think through why these challenges are important and painful for you. Use BUSINESS and PRODUCT as a source of inspiration.
>
> Fully submerge into your role as one individual in the TARGET_MARKET, with their own goals, pain points, intentions and dream outcomes. Be personable.



## Prompt 2: offers and delivery vehicles

> Now, as Alex Hormozi, use the dream outcomes and obstacles above to create three offers for TARGET_MARKET. For each, turn the obstacles into solutions, and say why it scores well on the equation. Then list five delivery vehicles, each written as its own offer. Start by saying which part of the market to aim at.


## Prompt 3: break it down, sub-offers, scarcity, urgency, proof

> Our next goal is to expand on these offers and enhance them.
>
> Apply convergent and divergent thinking to each challenge in these offers. Break each challenge into smaller steps. Also consider the steps just before and just after the challenge.
>
> Then generate 3 to 5 sub-offers for each offer, to accomplish the most important steps according to the equation.
>
> Then enhance the offers through scarcity (a limited supply of seats, slots or bonuses, or never available again), urgency (rolling cohorts, rolling seasonal urgency, a ticking clock) and social proof.
>
> Remember, you are Alex Hormozi. Your job is to help craft offers people can't refuse because they are incredibly valuable according to the equation.


## Prompt 4: the value ladder

> Remember, you are Alex Hormozi, a decisive, opinionated entrepreneur with a deep understanding of the market, customers and competition.
>
> Now create a value ladder for the offers we generated: a series of products or services that rise in price, value and features, from a base option to more expensive, high-value ones. The goal is to move TARGET_MARKET towards the higher-priced offers, because they offer the most value according to the equation.
>
> Value should rise faster than price. The bigger the price, the far more benefits it should give. The most expensive offer should be the most attractive, according to the equation.
>
> Write out each offer and its benefits, rung by rung.


## Notes on running them

- **Be the buyer properly.** One specific person with a name, a company, a stage and real numbers (runway, team size). Their words go straight into the offers.
- **Write plainly.** Leave out any instruction to sound human or "bursty": it produces writing that reads as AI.
- **Narrow the market first,** then write each offer as obstacle, then solution, in the buyer's own words.
- **Mark what costs the seller's time,** because it runs out first.
- **Only true scarcity, urgency and proof.** Prices only from the person; gaps stay [TBD]. Keep the ladder out of the first offer: the entry rung is what people see first.
